## The plan (draft for review)

From today's code to the model above, in stages. Each stage lands as commits that each keep `make check`, the suite and smoke green, with each new test run once with its piece removed. Before building, the decisions at the end are to be settled against the model (step 3).

### Already as the model says (on `verse/ask-next`)

- **The consequence runs on the raiser's authority alone** (2e17878e).
- **A person's ask** is its asker's record, written by its own verb (`verse ask`, `POST /asks`), read by its raiser and its parties, its question included (854b804b, 69a7093b), with a structured `then` (`prompt`), a `due`, and a keep after it settles (8451a6bc).
- **A task's question may carry a clock**, and a conversation turn has none (1d9306b5).
- **Answering by named parties**, a group answering by counting, and a claim (the first answer).
- **The raiser is told**: `verse ask --watch`.

### Where today's code differs

- **G1, one record.** An ask is two records today: the raiser's side (`verse.ask.<id>`, a rule's firing, a task) and the question on the docket (`verse.gate.question.<id>`). The model has one record.
- **G2, changes.** An ask has no revision, and answers name none. A person's ask takes no change but withdrawal. A rule's gate follows its subject: an edit re-fires it under the same question and runs what the subject says at settle, which is the consent gap found earlier.
- **G3, reading.** A rule's gate and a task's question are read by every reader of the docket.
- **G4, lifetime.** Only a person's ask goes after it settles. Settled gates and task questions stand.
- **G5, resolvers.** A resolver owns questions by their subject. The ladder is standing resolvers in name order, a hand-over moves `read-write` on the question, and escalating re-owns it. The model's resolver is a declared match with no overlap, a grant to answer for, linked onward asks, and nested clocks.
- **G6, the answering side's policy.** Who may raise to a party, and a resolver overriding the ask's policy (whether silence counts as yes): none today.
- **G7, two ways.** Words sent to a running task (`say`) are not an ask.
- **G8, then.** `prompt` only. `calls` is next.
- **G9, names.** gate, question, ask and rendezvous name overlapping things.
- **G10, doors.** The command line raises and answers. Slack posts and answers questions. MCP has none.
- **G11, reading at every door is exact** only once the permission check is ordered with the store (7q4azhtj). That waits on go-tony's `progress` (gkmdvps7), not yet released.

### Stages

1. **One ask record (G1, G9).** Every raiser writes the one record: a person, a rule's gate, a task. Its name and place are decision D1. The docket, `verse gate`, Slack and `verse ask` read it. The raiser's side keeps what only the raiser has (a rule's firing, a task's wait) and links to it.
2. **Revision and changes (G2).** A revision on the ask, and on each answer and ballot. The verb admits changes: the clock without asking again; what was asked by asking again, which drops earlier ballots. A rule's gate asks again when its subject changes what it poses or what follows, which closes the consent gap. This includes step 3 of the earlier design (`--due`, then `--then`, on a person's ask).
3. **Reading and lifetime for every ask (G3, G4).** Every ask is read by its raiser, its parties and the raiser's grantees, and goes after its keep once settled.
4. **Resolvers as subscription and party (G5).**
   - Declaration with a match over asks, and the overlap refusal.
   - The grant to answer for a person or a place.
   - Onward asks linked to the ask they serve, with nested clocks.
   - Escalating as an onward ask.
   - Then retiring ownership by subject, the name-ordered ladder, and hand-over by `read-write`.
5. **The answering side's policy (G6),** on a party's resolver: who may raise to it, and overriding silence.
6. **Two ways (G7).** Words to a running task are an ask to the task, answered at its next boundary, or as the answer where the task waits on that person.
7. **`then` (G8).** `calls`, through `callsFrom` with `as: subject`.
8. **Doors (G10).** Slack and MCP raise, answer and follow asks.

G11 goes beside the stages, when go-tony releases `progress`: the perms view's barrier becomes a coalesced `progress` on its watch (the uncommitted work in `.worktrees/perms-order`).

### Decisions to settle before building

- **D1, the record.** Where the one ask record lives (`verse.ask.<id>` for every raiser, or the docket's place), and what `verse gate` becomes beside `verse ask`.
- **D2, the branch.** Continue on `verse/ask-next`, which is unmerged and large, or merge it to `main` first and build the stages on `main`.
- **D3, a rule's reader.** A rule raises a gate. Who is its raiser's grantee for reading: the rule's installer, or the readers of what the gate is about?
- **D4, keep for rules and tasks.** The default keep of a rule's gate and a task's question once settled.
- **D5, a resolver's match.** What a match may name (about, to, raiser, more), and the granularity of the overlap check.
- **D6, migration.** Existing charters' gate `resolver:` fields and standing resolvers, under the new resolvers.
