## The model (draft for review)

Everything that asks for an answer in verse is an **ask**, the building block this proposal calls a rendezvous. This section says what an ask is and who takes part in one, in terms of the end state. How today's code differs from it belongs to the plan, not here.

### An ask

One record, with these parts:

- **raiser**: who asks. A person, a task or a rule.
- **to**: the parties it is put to. A party is a person, a group or a resolver.
- **about**: optionally, what it is about: a path in the verse.
- **words**: what is asked. Optionally, options to choose between; without options, the answer is in words.
- **clock**: when it settles, and what silence settles on. Both are part of the ask, set by the raiser. Without a clock, it waits until it is answered.
- **then**: what follows the answer: nothing, the raiser's next step, or a task. It runs on the raiser's authority alone.
- **outcome**: how it settled: an answer and who gave it, the clock, or a withdrawal.

An ask settles once. After it settles, anything more is a new ask.

### Parties

- A **person** raises and answers.
- A **task** raises (a question to its person) and answers (words sent to it, at its next boundary).
- A **rule** raises (a gate) and does not answer.
- A **group** answers by counting. Each member answers once, as themselves. The ask says how many answers settle it: one, a quorum, or the first to claim.
- A **resolver** answers on behalf of a place or a person. It answers itself, or it raises an ask of its own to another party and answers with what comes back.

### Who an ask goes to

1. The parties the raiser names, each through its own resolver where it has designated one.
2. Where it names none and is about something: that thing's resolver, the party its place designates to answer asks about it.
3. Where it names none and is about nothing: anyone who may read it. That is a vote.

### Resolvers

A resolver is how a place, or a person, says who answers for it. A place's owner designates one for asks about what stands there. A person designates one for asks put to them.

When an ask reaches a resolver, the resolver:

- answers it: a service, or a person's agent;
- raises an ask onward and answers with its outcome: delegation, and escalation when the onward ask goes up; or
- declines it, and the ask goes to the next resolver the place or person designates. Past the last one, it goes to the person themselves, or, for a place, to anyone who may read it.

A resolver **may override the ask's policy** for the party it serves. For example, it may not let silence count as its person's yes. The override is on the record of the outcome.

### Authority

- **Raising** needs authority to put an ask to those parties. That is the answering side's policy, which its resolver applies. Where there is none, anyone may ask.
- **Answering** is for the parties the ask is put to, directly or through their resolver. An answer is one party's, and is checked as theirs.
- **The consequence** runs on the raiser's authority alone. An answer is consent, not a grant. A yes never widens what `then` may do.

### Sequences

A sequence is one ask after another, each with its own clock, so each sequence ends.

- **Extending**: the raiser moves the clock of an ask that is still waiting. It is a change to the ask made through the verb that owns it, and it does not ask again. "Wednesday instead?" from the answerer is an ask back to the raiser.
- **Escalating**: when the clock passes, or a resolver passes it on, a new ask goes to the next party.
- **Claiming**: the first to answer settles it.

### Two ways

A task asks its person; a person asks a task. The words a person sends a running task are an ask to the task. If the task is waiting on that person, they are the answer. If not, they wait until the task's next boundary. A conversation is a sequence of these.

### Reading

An ask is read by its raiser and by the parties it is put to, and by whoever else the raiser grants. What it carries, its words included, is read no more widely than the ask.

### Lifetime

An ask stands while it waits, and for its keep once it has settled (set by the raiser, a week by default). Then it is removed. The record of what happened is the log's, kept as the log's retention says.

### Doors

Every door raises, answers and follows asks. Following is the second building block, a subscription: a cursored, one-way reading of a stream.

### What is not an ask

- A **query**, which waits for nobody.
- **Pause, resume, cancel**: one party acting alone on an ask or a task.
- A **subscription**.

### Exceptions

- A **conversation turn** has no clock (decided 2026-09-28).
