**Decided (2026-09-28): the view is a second building block, a subscription.** Attach does not become a rendezvous.

A **subscription** is a cursored, one-way reading of a subject's stream. It has no clock and no consequence. `verse watch`, `verse task attach`, `verse flow show` and a visualizer are all subscriptions. It pairs with the rendezvous: the subscription is for reading, and the rendezvous is for interacting. Both exist today: the stream and the cursor (`streamEvents`, and an attached resolver's cover and position).

## The alternatives considered

- **A. Attach is a door with presence.** While a person is attached, questions about that subject go there first.
- **B. An answerer names a session.** `answerers` may name an attached session, answered by whoever holds it.
- **C. Attach is a rendezvous.** "May I watch?" is raised and answered, and the consequence is a stream for a limited time.
- **D. The view is a second building block, a subscription.** Chosen.
- **E. A subscription plus presence.** Being attached is a record, and a rendezvous reads it to choose where to deliver and what silence means.

| | Building blocks | Reuses | New state |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1 | the door | routing by presence |
| B | 1 | the resolver's cover | a session as answerer |
| C | 1 | the rendezvous | a lease |
| D | 2 | the event stream and the cursor | none |
| E | 2 | D, and the rendezvous | a presence record |

## Why D

- **D adds no new state.** Verse already has too many moving parts, and at this stage each one costs a lot. E adds a presence record and its expiry, so it is rejected for this reason.
- **A adds routing state.** Verse is near the limit of the routing state it can carry, and that state will grow when authentication works.
- **B and C are errors of UX design.** C puts a question where the permission model already answers, so people are asked more for no gain. B makes an answerer something that disappears, which a rendezvous with a clock cannot rely on.

A conversation turn keeps no clock. That is an exception, and we accept it.
