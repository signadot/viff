## The order of work

Each step leaves the tree green and is worth having on its own, because we are still feeling this
out end to end. Steps 1–4 are the words; 5–6 are answering in prose; 7 is the shape the rest is
built to fit.

Two spellings of a reference will coexist while this lands — `pose: !eval "$[subject.value.sha]"`
beside `patch: {sha: $subject.value.sha}` — on the reading that tony's eval is where the grammar
is heading, and verse's own deref is what converges on it rather than the other way round.

**1. Prose on an option.** `task.Option` gains `says`; `optionList` writes it onto the record
(`mirror/question.go:421`); the charter grammar takes it; the Slack io labels a button with it and
falls back to the id. *Proves:* the record can carry words a presenter shows, on the path that
already works.

**2. `pose` on a gate, rendered by the engine.** `Gate` gains `pose`; the engine expands it at
fire time with go-tony's eval trimmed to expressions, over an environment of the subject
(`trigger/engine.go:887` is where the subject and the gate meet); `GateQuestion` carries the
rendered string; `PostQuestion` writes it. Expansion failing keeps `reason`, records the error and
asks anyway. A task's own gate gets the same through the deref that already expands a spawn's
string fields, so `task` learns nothing new. *Proves:* the question says what the rule saw, at the
moment it saw it, and a broken sentence still reaches somebody.

**3. `about` filled at posting.** Versed reads the subject at both post paths
(`versed/server.go:375` and `:424`) and copies its `says`, trimmed to a line as `verse docket`
already does (`06476112`). *Proves:* a presenter holding no grant on the subject can still say
what the question is about — which is the Slack io today.

**4. The message reads as prose.** The io's layout becomes `pose` (falling back to `reason`), then
`about` and the subject, the deadline, who it waits on; buttons labelled from step 1; a footer
saying how to answer in words. The notification text carries the question, since a block renders
in fewer places than a notification does. *Proves:* the message is readable, and answerable, where
blocks are not.

**5. Answering by thread reply.** Needs the app's Event Subscriptions, which nothing has switched
on; the socket already carries the deliveries (`81cea076`). The io indexes its own statuses by
`ts`, so a reply's parent names the question exactly; the text is matched against the option ids
plus `y`/`n` aliases for the first two; the answer is made as the person who typed it, and the io
replies in the thread saying what it did. A reply matching nothing is left alone until step 6.
*Proves:* a gate can be answered with no interactive component at all — mobile, a client that
renders no blocks, a person who types faster than they click.

**6. A say on a question.** `/gate/resolve` refuses an answer with no choice (`versed/server.go:2815`),
and rightly; a comment is a different act. A route of its own appends `said: [{by, at, text}]` to
the record, an unmatched thread reply becomes one, and the io shows the last few beneath the
message. *Proves:* the conversation lands on the record, attributed, without deciding anything —
and gives step 7 something to fall into when it cannot tell.

**7. `@verse approve it pls`.** Not claimed here, and the reason the earlier steps are shaped as
they are. Three invariants hold whatever interprets the text:

- **Context is resolved without language.** A thread gives the question exactly. A mention with no
  thread has a candidate set — what that channel waits on from that person — and anything other
  than exactly one is asked back, never guessed.
- **The option ids are the whole action space.** An interpreter picks one of the ids on that
  record, or says it cannot tell (step 6). It never invents an effect, which is "a button can only
  answer a question" carried into prose.
- **Identity stays the person's.** The io asserts whoever typed, and verse's permissions decide.
  An interpreter is a parser, never the answerer — and if it ever runs as a verse task, that
  ordering is what keeps a person's approval from becoming an agent's.
