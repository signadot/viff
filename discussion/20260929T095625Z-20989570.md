**Stage 4: two questions before serving and answering as a party.**

**S1. How does a resolver's service act as the resolver?** Today a url resolver's service reads and answers through a **cover**. A cover is a system-minted id for one resolver on one stream, and it works as a capability: the routes are unattributed, and the cover id is the credential. Stage 4 retires covers. Principals are self-asserted, and a request may not present `via:` itself.
- **(a) The installer presents it.** The service sends its installer's principal (`user:ana`) plus a header naming the resolver. Verse acts as `via:verse.resolvers.<R>` only if that principal owns R's record. This fits "a resolver acts as whoever installed it", and adds no secret beyond what principals already are.
- **(b) A minted capability per resolver.** Verse mints a token at install, shown once to the installer, and the service presents it. This keeps what covers had, an unguessable credential, without per-stream records.

I recommend (a). Verse has no login, so a token is only as strong as the channel that showed it. (b) is worth it only if we want resolvers to be stronger than people.

**S2. How does a resolver read the asks it serves?** An ask's `read` line today is the asker and answerers, or `audience:<subject>`. The resolver serving it holds neither. Stage 4 says an `answer` grant also admits reading the asks put to that party, or about that place.
- **(a) Put it on the ask's record.** A new grantee, `answering:<address>`, admits whoever holds `answer` there. When an ask is posed, its `read` line gains `answering:verse.for.<P>` for each named party P, and `answering:<subject>` for a place. It follows grants as they change, as `audience:` does, and is the same one-level lookup.
- **(b) A special case in the read check for `verse.ask`.**

I recommend (a). It's data on the record, visible through `verse perms`, and it's the pattern `audience:` set.

With those, the next commits are:
- `Serving(ask, party)`;
- `GET /resolvers/{name}/asks`, a watch over the asks R serves, with a cursor;
- answering as R for P: `via:R` must hold `answer` at P's node, and the ballot is P's with `via: R`.
