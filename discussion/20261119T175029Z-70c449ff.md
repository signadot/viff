## What was decided, and built

The body above is the state this was filed against. The answer to its question is yes, with hooks: an uninstall stops the package's work while its rules still stand, so each task's end is taken as any end is.

As built on the task-end branches (`internal/versed/uninstall.go`, `internal/trigger/stop.go`):

1. The package is locked. Nothing of it is launched and its charter is not changed. An install at the address, or the removal of one rule, level or schedule of it, is refused with `429`, to be asked again.
2. Its tasks are cancelled, each asked until it takes the cancel. Each end runs its `onCancelled`, its claim comes off, its sequence ends. A hook that would launch is refused whole.
3. Its rules, levels, schedules and records are removed, with its engine.
4. The lock comes off.

Against the list in the body:

- **A cancel that fails** is asked again. The uninstall stays at step 2, the package installed and locked, until the task takes it or ends. A runner that cannot be reached ends the task at its lease (zm4kqgzsh12krkmtznn0, stages 4 and 5), so step 2 ends.
- **Uninstall then install** does not carry running tasks across. An install over a standing package still does.
- **Prune paths** go through the same uninstall.
- **Hooks** run, as the rule's record, which is still there at step 2.
- **A parked launch** is dropped at the lock, ended as cancelled.

A verse that restarts during step 2 comes back with the package installed and not locked, for the uninstall to be asked again. A package that is gone when verse comes back has its tasks cancelled with no hook, as before: no rule is there to say what the end owes.
