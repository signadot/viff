The verse side of the read, measured. The short of it: verse takes WHOLE-VERSE snapshots on the
firing path, their cost grows with the store, and at a few thousand commits they are already seconds —
on a trajectory that crosses the 10s read budget outright. Narrowing, which the store's own
comment says is pointless, is 1500× cheaper and flat.

## What verse asks for

Two shapes, and only one of them is narrowed.

- `Engine.evaluate` (trigger/edge.go:77, :90) reads `AsOf(c.Rev, paths(t.reads())...)` — one
  `MatchAt` per subtree the rule reads, per (rule, commit), plus a second AsOf at C-1 when the
  rule has an `on`. NARROWED.
- The firing's snapshot (trigger/trigger.go:504, `occasion.snapshot`) reads `AsOf(oc.commit.Rev)`
  with NO `within` — the whole verse. Taken lazily, once per firing, whenever an action uses
  `$subject.value` or a path read. `covering: !get-path {at: git.verse.head, path: sha}` — the
  drift claim rule's own action — takes one.
- `$subject.value` for a fan-out member (trigger/deref.go:477) reads `e.store.AsOf(oc.commit.Rev)`
  directly, bypassing the `snapCache` above: one whole-verse read PER MEMBER.

`select.go:314` is narrowed. Those three are the whole of the AsOf surface.

## What each costs

Measured on an in-process store, entities of four small fields (real github documents are
far bigger, so this is a floor, not an estimate):

    commits   AsOf(root)   AsOf(within)
       1000      161ms         ~80ms
       2000     1262ms          1.8ms
       3000     2421ms         37.0ms
       4000     3617ms         38.6ms
       5000     5426ms         40.2ms
       6000     6259ms         39.5ms

The narrowed read is FLAT in the tens of ms and independent of the log; the whole-root read grows
about 1.2ms per commit past 2000. At ~9,500 commits it exceeds `sessionBudget` (10s) on its own,
with no contention at all.

This contradicts the comment at `LogdStore.AsOf`, which says `within` "bounds what crosses the
wire, and — measured — that is ALL it does … so do not reach for this expecting a read to get
cheaper." That was true of some earlier vintage; it is not true now, and it is load-bearing in
the wrong direction: docs/working/firing-residual.md §2 lists `within` as "a candidate for
removal, since measurement says bounding the path is all it does". It is the opposite — it is the
cheap fix.

## What it is not

Two hypotheses tested and rejected, so nobody re-walks them:

- NOT head-of-line blocking. With four whole-root reads in flight, a narrowed read still returned
  in 4.9ms mean / 11.6ms worst against 1.8ms alone. Reads do not queue behind each other.
- NOT write contention on its own. Under a continuous write stream, narrowed reads ran 23.8ms
  mean / 29.5ms worst — 20× slower, still three orders off the budget.

What remains is the whole-verse read itself: seconds of server work each, several in flight
during a storm, on a docd with a CPU limit that a laptop does not have. That is what a 10s budget
gets spent on, and the drift rule's own cheap read is collateral.

## What to do, cheapest first

1. **Narrow the firing snapshot** (trigger.go:504). The rule's reads are already computed for
   evaluate; the action's path reads resolve at fire time. 1500× at today's size, and it keeps
   getting better as the store grows.
2. **Share it across a fan-out** (deref.go:477 → the `snapCache`). A fan-out of 20 members reading
   `$subject.value` is 20 whole-verse reads today, unshared, at seconds each.
3. **Fix the `AsOf` comment and the firing-residual note**, which currently tell the next person
   that 1 and 2 are pointless.
4. The roll-forward (firing-residual §2) is still the real answer — it makes `store@C` free and
   `store@(C-1)` already in hand. 1 and 2 are what to do while it is not built.

None of this changes that a read which missed its budget is taken as "nothing selected" and the
commit is dropped — that is this issue. But it is the read side the direction asks for, and the
first two items are small.
