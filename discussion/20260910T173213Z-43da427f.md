## `--override` is written for somebody who already knows

    -override  answer over an exclusive resolver; the record names you both

(cmd/verse/client.go:816 and :939, cmd/verse/task.go:342.)

Why would I know this gate has an exclusive resolver, and that it is not me? Nothing tells me.
The docket does not mark an exclusively-owned question — deliberately, and the reason is good:
'exclusive governs whose answer COUNTS. Not who may SEE the question: the docket is unchanged,
and a person may read — and argue about — what an exclusive binding owns' (versed/server.go).
`verse gate <id>` does not carry it either: the owner is derived from the resolver bindings at
answer time, not stored on the question. So there is no surface, before answering, on which the
condition this flag is about appears at all.

The one place it IS disclosed is the refusal, and that part is well done:

    403: verse.resolvers.security.payments-review answers this question exclusively;
         say override to answer it anyway, and the record will name you both

Which means the real flow is: answer, get told, then decide whether to override. The flag's
help is written the other way round — as a thing you reach for knowingly, naming a concept
(`exclusive resolver`) that a person reading `verse gate --help` has no reason to have met and
no way to check against the gate in front of them. Reading it cold, the natural questions are
'is this about my gate?' and 'how would I find out?', and the help answers neither.

Same shape as the rest of this issue: the model is right and the surface assumes the reader
shares it. Options, none of which touch the deliberate decision to leave the docket alone:

  - say it in terms of the failure, not the concept: 'answer anyway when told somebody else
    answers this exclusively (the refusal names them; the record names you both)';
  - or make ownership visible on the record when it exists, so the flag has an antecedent;
  - or drop the flag from the general help and let the 403 introduce it, since the refusal
    already tells you the exact thing to say.
