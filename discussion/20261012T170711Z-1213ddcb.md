**Sandboxing at launch (decided with Scott; the plan's steps are unchanged).**

- **Today:** under `verse up`, the verse agent (`driver: llm`) and scripts run with no sandbox: in `versed`'s process, the agent's shell a plain `bash -lc` (`verserunner/agent/exec.go`). Only the deployed coderunner confines a task, with bwrap around a process per task (`hop.go`, `confine.go`).
- **A central deployment always sandboxes:** standalone `runnerd` runs every task in its own process under bwrap, for `verse`, scripts, and later `claude` and `codex` alike (our own bwrap, not the vendor's sandbox). It refuses to start without bwrap, as the coderunner does with `-confine bwrap`, so nothing runs unconfined by accident.
- **`verse up` doesn't sandbox at all:** an embedded runner runs with no confiner, on every OS. That covers verse-runner, and later `claude` and `codex` with their own sandboxes off. What a task may do is still governed by autonomy and step gates (2c), which are separate from the sandbox.
- **So in step 1b:** one process per task under bwrap applies only to standalone `runnerd`, and the macOS question goes away (`verse up` doesn't sandbox; central is Linux). The per-task process and the confiner live in `runnerd`, not `verserunner`, so `claude-runner` and `codex-runner` reuse them.
- **Under `verse up`, `network` (2b) isn't enforced:** a launch asking for `none` runs and is logged as unconfined, not refused, so a charter that works centrally works locally.
- This is for the launch; local sandboxing can be improved later (bwrap on Linux; the vendors' Seatbelt on macOS for `claude`/`codex`).
