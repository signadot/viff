**Stage 4, S3: where a resolver's context comes from once covers are retired.**

A cover does two jobs:
- it is a credential, which S1 replaced;
- it is the **spine**: `GET /resolvers/cover/{id}/events` streams the frames of the run that raised the question, or the history of a firing's subject, from a cursor. The code comment states the point: "context arrives before the ask, which is the whole reason an answerer is attached rather than handed a question".

Covers are minted when a spawn or a rule names a resolver, or when a standing resolver takes a question. Those three go with the ladder, so the spine needs a new home.

- **(a) Read as the installer.** A resolver reads context through the ordinary routes (`/watch` on the subject, the task's events) as `via:` the resolver, which reads whatever its installer reads.
  - A person's own resolver, installed by them, sees their runs and their places.
  - A team's resolver sees what its installer sees.
  - No new grant. The cursor is `/watch`'s `since`.
- **(b) The ask admits its origin.** Where an ask is raised by a task, the task's `read` line also carries `answering:` for the ask's parties, so a resolver granted by a party reads the run that asked it. Wider than (a) for a resolver installed by someone who can't read the run, and permanent on the task once asked.
- **(c) Keep covers as context only.** Mint a per-ask cursor for each resolver serving the ask, with no ownership.

I recommend (a). It follows "a resolver acts as whoever installed it" and adds nothing. It does mean a resolver someone else installed, granted by bob, answers bob's asks with only what that installer can read, beyond the ask itself.

Until you decide, I'm retiring what doesn't depend on this: standing ownership, the escalation ladder, and `exclusive`. Covers, the spine and the gate and spawn `resolver:` fields wait.
