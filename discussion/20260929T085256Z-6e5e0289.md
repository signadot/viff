**D3 revised: an ask about a subject is read and answered as its subject is (`audience:<subject>`).**

The rule underneath: nobody can answer an ask about something they can't read.

- **The grantee.** `audience:<address>` goes in a grant's `read` or `read-write` list. In a list, it admits whoever holds that list's level at the address. It is allowed nowhere else: owning the subject never makes you the ask's owner, and control never carries over either. It is looked up one level deep only: an `audience:` reached while answering an `audience:` admits nobody. It is a set, never a principal. It replaces the `readers:` from the first cut.
- **No named answerers.** The grants are `read: [audience:S]` and `read-write: [audience:S]`. Whoever may read S reads the ask, and whoever may write S answers it. Both follow S's permissions as they stand at each check.
- **Named answerers.** The grants are `read: [audience:S]` and `read-write: <answerers>`. Each answerer must be able to read S:
  - When the ask is posed, it errors: a person's ask gets a 403 from POST /asks, and a rule gate's firing or a task's action fails.
  - When an answer arrives, the answer is refused.
  - A group is expanded. A kind (`user:*`) passes only if S's own grants give read to that kind or to `*`.
  - A resolver the ask is handed to is held to the same check.
- **"You can't answer your own ask"** stays separate from grants (`askedBy`, the runner rule).
- **An ask with no subject** keeps the docket's grants, as before.
