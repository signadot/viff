## The model, revised: "Who an ask goes to" and "Resolvers"

These two sections replace the ones in the draft above. The rest of the draft stands. The change: a resolver is the two building blocks together, following asks and answering them, so the ladder is only matching (2026-09-29).

### Who an ask goes to

An ask is put to parties, or is about something, or both.

1. The resolver whose match covers the ask answers it, where it answers for the ask's parties, or for the place the ask is about.
2. Otherwise the parties answer it themselves.
3. An ask put to nobody, and served by no resolver, is a vote: anyone who may read it may answer.

### Resolvers

A resolver is the two building blocks together: a **subscription** over asks, and a **party** that answers asks and raises them.

- **Declared, with a match.** A resolver is declared with a match over asks: what they are about, whom they are put to, who raised them. verse refuses a resolver whose match overlaps one already declared, as it refuses a source whose slice overlaps another's. So at most one resolver serves any ask.
- **What it does.** Following its subscription, it answers an ask; or it raises an onward ask and answers with what that settles on; or it leaves the ask to its parties.
- **Seeing is not answering.** Its match decides which asks it follows. Its answer counts only for those who granted it to answer for them: a person, for asks put to them; a place's owner, for asks about the place. The grant is a record of authority, like `read`, not routing.
- **Linked asks.** An onward ask carries a link to the ask it serves, which makes the two a sequence. The link is how the outcome is carried back, and how anyone reading, over an attach, sees why the onward ask exists.
- **Clocks nest.** An onward ask's clock ends no later than what is left on the ask it serves, so a chain of resolvers cannot outlast the first ask.
- **No ladder to keep.** Escalating is raising an onward ask. The next rung is whichever resolver's match covers that ask. No order is stored anywhere.
- **Overriding.** A resolver may override the ask's policy for those it answers for, for example not letting silence count as its person's yes. The override is on the record of the outcome.
