FIXED as far as it can be from here, `7b6cb09d` — and the flake turned out to have a defect
under it rather than a margin.

REPRODUCED DELIBERATELY first, since "fails under whatever load the whole-repo run puts on the
machine" is not something to fix blind: six `go test ./trigger` in the background with
GOMAXPROCS=1 gives about 5 failures in 20 on `TestOverrideNamesBothParties`. The record it fails
on is the finding:

    status:pending  verdict:proceed  by:human:…  answeredAt:…  overrode:…

A question to decide, already stamped with who decided it and what they said.

WHY. A local run posts the question TWICE — once from the registry, once from its frame.
`PostFrameQuestion`'s own comment says so and argues the two agree on every field they share.
They do not agree on `status` once an answer lands between them: the frame's write merges
`pending` over `answered` and leaves the verdict underneath. `PostQuestion` guards this with
`clearPriorAnswer`; the frame path had no guard at all.

`status` and `posedAt` are conditional now — written only while the record does not already say
it settled. The precondition asks about the whole payload (`!not {status: …}`) so it is also
true for a record with no status yet, which is the brokered case.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN, measured rather than assumed. The guard does not CLOSE the race. Probed with
both writers instrumented:

    PROBE answer      id=run-1~d1
    PROBE frame-posed id=run-1~d1 werr=<nil> now-status=pending
    PROBE answer      id=run-1~d1

The write was admitted against a record that had just been answered — the precondition is
evaluated against a view that lags the answer by a few milliseconds. Reading-then-branching
narrowed it too, and also did not close it. What is left needs the two paths ORDERED rather than
each checking, which is a question about the runtime, so I am leaving this issue open for it
rather than closing on a partial fix.

THE SUITE IS NOT FLAKY EITHER WAY. Both tests wait for the record to settle now
(`settledQuestion`), which is what every sibling test in the package already does — the two that
flaked read the instant a route returned 204. That is this issue's own prescription: the boundary
is observable, and the margin is gone. Clean across 40 loaded runs of every gate test in the
package and two full loaded runs of `./versed`.

The second test named in the discussion, `TestOverrideNamesBothParties`, is the one this was all
about; `TestAnsweringBeforeTheDeadline` had the same unwaited read and is fixed with it.
