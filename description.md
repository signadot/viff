# docs: say from the splash on, and throughout, that verse is a shared space

A reader of the site took verse for a personal tool: "I'm a developer ... I want to automate my delivery workflow ... I am the orchestrator" (a LinkedIn exchange, 2026-10-24). Told that verse is a shared space, adopted by a coding organization, where everything runs under the users' identities and anyone with the permission can steer any agent, the same reader called it a game changer. The site never says so. The splash (docs/README.md) says "Verse runs software delivery work for you", and the tutorial runs on a verse on your own machine with `--as` to stand for other people, which reads as a single-user tool unless the pages say what the local verse stands in for.

Say it from the splash on: verse is one shared verse for a team or an organization, that everyone and every agent in it acts under a user's identity, that asks go to named people and anyone they are put to answers from anywhere, that tasks are observable and steerable by whoever holds the permission, and that the single-machine verse of the tutorial is a stand-in for that. Carry the framing through the concepts page, the tutorial pages, the comparison and the glossary, in a sentence or two where each page's subject meets it, not as a new section everywhere.
