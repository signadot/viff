Verse#

Verse is a shared space where a team's software delivery work runs: agents, scripts and people in one verse, and the team decides how much of the work runs without asking.

Verse watches the systems the work lives in: a git repository, GitHub, Slack, Linear, an issue tracker. When something there changes, verse does what your rules say. It starts an agent on a checkout, opens a pull request, posts a message, or labels an issue. Before a step that matters, it asks the people the rule names, and nothing happens until they answer.

Verse is not a programming language, and it is not an agent. It is a small runtime. It coordinates the agents and tools you already have, under rules you write, and it keeps a record of every decision. The rules are the same for one agent or for many.

One verse is shared by a team or an organization. Everything in it is done under a name, a person's, an agent's or a runner's: a write, a task, an answer, each says who. A question goes to the people it names, and they answer from wherever they are: the CLI, Slack, a pull request. A task one person started is visible to everyone who may read it, and whoever holds control over it steers it. Verse is not a personal automation, and no one person is its orchestrator.
