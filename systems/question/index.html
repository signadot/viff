question#

The question system is the questions that a task puts to people. The agent or script hands the turn over and waits on the docket for an answer. A task that asks as the person it runs for, and goes on, uses the verse system instead.

It is not the question a runner asks before a step, when an autonomy row of the task says ask. That question comes from the autonomy, not from a call of the task.

What a charter sees#

Every question is a record at verse.ask.<id>, whoever asked it: a person with verse ask, a rule's gate, or a task with this tool. A rule reacts to one as it reacts to any data. A charter installed at the system level is what carries the docket to people in Slack (asking).

What a charter writes#

A rule asks in two ways: with a gate before it acts, and with an ask action, whose yes runs what it names (asking). Neither is a verb of this system. This system is how a task asks.

What a task can do#

A task has question-ask when its environment gives question, as it has any tool. Asking a person is the step question.ask, and the autonomy row that matches it decides it: allow puts the question at once, deny refuses it, and ask asks before the question is put. Codex's and Claude's own questions for a person are the same step, and Claude is offered its question tool only where the environment gives question.

spawn:
  agent: {name: verse}
  environment: {systems: {network: {mode: open}, question: {}}}
  prompt: "Decide whether $[subject.ref] can be rolled back, and ask before you say so."
  autonomy:
  - match: {system: question}
    then: allow
  - then: deny
Tool What it does Step
question-ask puts a question to the person the task runs for, or to the answerers it names, and waits; the answer is the call's result. With options, the answer is one of them. With within, silence settles on default. question.ask

The question is the task's, and it is on the docket beside every other question. verse question lists it, and the answer given there is what the tool returns. In a conversation, the agent asks after every answer, to give the person the turn. A script asks with verse-call-tool question-ask, and reads the answer on its standard output.

Where the credential lives#

Nowhere. A question is verse's own record. The runner poses it, and the answer comes back through verse. No system outside verse is reached, so no credential is needed.
