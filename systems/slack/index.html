slack#

The slack system is the channels of a Slack workspace that a bot is in. A charter reads the channels and writes to them through the iod. A task posts, replies, reads and reacts in them through its runner.

What a charter sees#

iod slack writes into the store each channel the bot is in, a window of each channel's recent messages, and the workspace's people:

Address What it is
slack.<ws>.channel.<C…> a channel: its name, whether it is private or archived, its topic
slack.<ws>.message.<C…>."<ts>" a message in the window: its text, its author, its thread and its reactions
slack.<ws>.user.<U…> a person: their name and email

A rule reacts to a message as it reacts to any data:

condition:
  subject:
    path: "slack.acme.message.C0456.*"
    value: {reactions: {shipit: null}}

iod slack has the flags, the scopes and the whole payload.

What a charter writes#

A message. With -out, the iod carries a rule's writes back to Slack: it posts what a rule writes under slack.<ws>.out (the out half). So a rule can say something in Slack with no task. This is also how a question reaches a person in Slack, and how the person's answer comes back.

What a task can do#

A task gets Slack from its runner, when the runner holds a bot token, and from its environment: a slack block gives the verbs, its shapes the channels. The autonomy decides each call within it:

spawn:
  agent: {name: verse}
  environment:
    systems:
      network: {mode: open}
      git: [{repo: acme/app, from: main, fetch: {}}]
      slack: {post: {channel: deploys}, read: {channel: deploys}}
  prompt: "Draft the release notes for v1.3 from the commits since v1.2, post them in #deploys, then read the thread for objections."
  autonomy:
  - {match: {system: slack, verb: post, channel: deploys}, then: ask}
  - {match: {system: slack, verb: read, channel: deploys}, then: allow}
  - {match: {system: slack}, then: deny}
  - {then: ask}
Tool What it does Step
slack-post posts a message in a channel, and returns its ts and link slack.post, with the param channel
slack-reply replies in a thread, under the message whose ts is the thread's slack.reply, with channel and thread
slack-read reads a channel's recent messages, or a thread's, newest last slack.read, with channel and thread
slack-react adds a reaction to a message, by its ts slack.react, with channel and emoji

Every verb is a step, a read too. What a channel holds is not the task's until the task reads it, and an environment that gives no read of a channel keeps it from the task.

A channel is named by its name, without the #: deploys, never its id. The runner finds the name among the channels the bot is in. The channel the environment and the autonomy decided about is the channel the runner posts in.

Every message a task writes ends with a line that names the task, verse task <id>. All tasks on a runner post as one bot, so the line says which task wrote each message. The iod reads the line with the message, so a rule can tie a message back to its task. A reaction has no such line.

A script calls the same tools with verse-call-tool, and the same rows decide them:

verse-call-tool slack-post -channel deploys -text "v1.3 is rolling out"

Where the credential lives#

The iod needs a bot token, SLACK_BOT_TOKEN, with the scopes that the iod reference lists.

The task holds no credential. The runner makes each call with its own bot token: the SLACK_BOT_TOKEN. Without it, a runner whose configuration names slack in a doer's ceiling starts with the system closed, and a spawn that needs it is refused where it is written. The bot needs chat:write; channels:read and groups:read to find a channel by its name; channels:history and groups:history to read; and reactions:write to react. The iod and the runner may use the same bot's token or two bots' tokens. Either way, a bot reaches only the channels it is in.

The operator sets a limit on what any task may do in Slack, in the runner's configuration, under each doer whose tasks may use it:

agents:
  claude:
    systems:
      slack:
        ceiling:
          post: {channel: deploys}
          reply: {channel: deploys}
          read: [{channel: deploys}, {channel: !glob "eng-*"}]
          react: {channel: deploys}

Verse refuses to install a spawn whose environment gives posting to another channel. The refusal says what the ceiling gives (the verse runner). A call is still held to the ceiling when the runner makes it, whatever the autonomy said.
