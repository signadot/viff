Tutorial -- Questions#

A question in verse is an ask. It is put to the people who may answer it. It can have a date, and it can say what a yes runs.

There are four ways to ask:

Who asks How
you verse ask, on this page
a task, for itself when it reaches something it may not do alone, and it waits for the answer (tasks)
a task, for you with its verse-ask-create tool, when its environment gives it the verse system. The ask is yours, and the task does not wait for it
a rule at its gate (charters)

All four make the same record, and every pending ask is on one list, the docket.

This page needs a verse running, and nothing else.

Ask#

verse ask deploy -q "Deploy the release to staging?" --to ana@example.com --due 1h \
  --on '[{match: {verdict: proceed}, then: {spawn: {script: "echo deploying", environment: {systems: {network: {mode: open}}}}}}]'
# raised question deploy-b8a7d6bea9e3: Deploy the release to staging?
Flag What it says
-q the question
--to who may answer. Without it, anyone may.
--due when it settles if nobody answers, as a date or a duration from now. Without it, it waits until somebody answers.
--on what runs after the answer, on your authority: rows, each a match on the answer and a then. This one row meets a yes ({verdict: proceed}), and its then is a spawn, written as a charter's is. Here it is a script, run as written, with the host's network; a prompt gives the job to an agent instead, and autonomy says what it may do unasked. Without --on, the ask only records the answer.

The name, deploy, is yours. The id adds a suffix, and commands take either one.

Who sees it#

verse ask                 # your asks: the question, who is asked, the date, the answer or pending
verse question            # the asks waiting on you: none, because you asked Ana
verse question --all      # the docket: every pending ask you can read

- deadline:
    at: "2026-10-18T01:08:12Z"
    default: deny
  from:
    principal: user:you@example.com
  id: deploy-b8a7d6bea9e3
  options:
  - id: proceed
    need: 1
  - id: deny
    need: 1
  question: "Deploy the release to staging?"
  to:
    grantees:
    - user:ana@example.com
  waitingOn:
  - user:ana@example.com
  …

The options are the two answers, proceed and deny, and each needs one vote. The deadline is what --due asked for. If nobody answers by then, the answer is no.

Answer#

Only somebody the ask is put to may answer. In a shared verse, Ana answers from her own CLI, or from Slack, where a charter has the Slack iod carry the question to her and her reply back. On a verse on your own machine, --as says who is answering, and verse takes the name as given; a deployment authenticates you instead. 

verse question deploy-b8a7d6bea9e3 --answer proceed --as bob@example.com
# answer proceed: 403 Forbidden: refused: user:bob@example.com holds nothing at verse.ask.deploy-b8a7d6bea9e3 …
verse question deploy-b8a7d6bea9e3 --answer proceed --as ana@example.com
# answered deploy-b8a7d6bea9e3
verse ask deploy          # answer: yes

Bob is not asked, so he may not answer. He may not even read the ask. Ana's yes met the row, which ran its spawn as a task that acts for you:

verse task

- script: true
  id: deploy-b8a7d6bea9e3-74645a658d25
  result: deploying
  status: done
  …

A task is the next page. Here it is enough that a yes can start one.

Silence, and changing your mind#

If nobody answers by the date, the ask settles on deny, unless you say otherwise. An ask for objections says otherwise:

verse ask hotfix -q "Any objection to the hotfix?" --to dee@example.com --due 10s --on-silence proceed
verse ask hotfix          # ten seconds later: answer: yes

While an ask waits, you can change it or take it back. Ask about the production deploy, give it more time, and then withdraw it:

verse ask deploy-prod -q "Deploy the release to production?" --to ana@example.com --due 1h
verse ask deploy-prod --due 4h         # moved deploy-prod-… to the date four hours from now
verse ask deploy-prod --withdraw

A new date moves the clock and keeps the answers given. A new --on or a new --to changes what was asked. So the ask is asked again, and the earlier answers do not count.

A question is data#

An ask is data at verse.ask.<id>, so the verbs of data read it:

verse data verse ask deploy-b8a7d6bea9e3 -trim '{answer, by, verdict, ballots}'

answer: yes
ballots:
  user:ana@example.com:
    at: "2026-10-18T00:08:18Z"
    authenticated: asserted
    choice: proceed
    edit: 1
by: user:ana@example.com
verdict: proceed

Each answer is a ballot. It says who chose what, and whether verse authenticated them or took their word. verse watch verse ask '*' follows every ask you can read, and a rule can select an ask as it selects any other data.

Asking has the rest: asks that answer other asks, a row for a no or for silence, how long an ask stands, and who may read it. Next, tasks.
